# Orra Story Bible v0.1

**Status: draft. This Story Bible is not active.**

## Premise

Orra is a city that wakes each season in a different landscape without knowing who moves it or why. Buildings, inhabitants, possessions, and promises travel with it; geography, resources, neighbors, and weather change. For the first recorded time, Orra has failed to reach the sea.

Season question: **what must a community remember to remain itself when its environment keeps changing?**

Orra is the recurring hub, not the whole world. Each destination may support distinct cultures, ecosystems, moods, characters, and local stories. Consequences can travel with Orra, remain behind, or return in a later season.

## World laws

1. Orra changes destination only at a declared season boundary.
2. Something established as remaining behind does not reappear in Orra without a causal return.
3. Consequences persist. Dreams, simulations, time travel, omnipotent magic, and apocalypse cannot reset prior play.
4. Strange phenomena may be wondrous, but repeated behavior becomes a world law and must remain consistent.
5. A character knows only what the public story gives that character reason to know.
6. An established motive can change through pressure, evidence, relationship, or experience, but not by assertion alone.
7. No player owns a character. Shared authorship does not permit unexplained personality replacement.

## Scale

Moves may operate at three scales:

- `CHARACTER`: a choice, relationship, discovery, or personal cost;
- `LOCAL`: a district, faction, resource, journey, or regional conflict;
- `WORLD`: a durable change to Orra or the wider setting.

Chapter Zero uses one spotlight storyline. Later chapters may support several named arcs, but each active arc needs its own bounded state and explicit relation to the world ledger.

## Tone

- wondrous and sensory, with concrete material and relational consequences;
- melancholic but hopeful rather than default grimdark;
- open to humor and tenderness, not disposable parody;
- mysterious without arbitrary mystery-box answers;
- suitable for a public mixed audience, without graphic violence or sexual content.

## Palette

Encouraged:

- places, weather, and infrastructure with strange but consistent behavior;
- choices among preservation, adaptation, departure, and belonging;
- recurring characters whose relationships change through events;
- small physical details that can become important much later;
- several defensible responses rather than one computable optimum.

Excluded as easy shortcuts:

- revealing that the world is an AI system, computer, log, simulation, or hallucination;
- turning governance, verification, or platform mechanics into the fiction by default;
- adding a secret world law solely to make one move succeed;
- erasing a settled cost through resurrection, reset, or universal catastrophe;
- importing real people, private context, or unverified external claims.

## Regional coherence

Every destination receives a region card naming its mood, sensory motifs, local oddity, central conflict, connection to Orra, and forbidden shortcut. Regions should feel different while obeying this same Bible.

One season may use three broad movements at a destination: arrival, choice, and departure or consequence. Chapters need not follow that pattern mechanically, but a new region is not a new universe.
